Demiurges follows the rules of the original games. It runs on modern systems and changes only what this page lists.

## Added

- Both games in one program, switched from the main menu, each with its own rules, saves and settings.
- Windows, Linux, macOS, Android and the browser; touch controls.
- [Remake settings](options): soft shadows, terrain detail, sharpening, anti-aliasing and more.
- The automatic camera can be interrupted by moving the camera yourself.
- This wiki, in English and Russian, in the game and on the web.

## Not available

- GameSpy and full strategic multiplayer. Native builds offer [network duels and a strategic preview](duel).
- Loading saves of the original games. Original Etherlords II replays can be watched.
- Intro and ending videos in the browser.

## Not yet exact

- Computer opponents follow the original's approach but are not an exact copy of its decisions.
- The darkening of a few large spells, the timing of some ability effects and Etherlords II map particles are approximations.
- The remake's own screens follow the language of your game. A few rare error messages and the battle log are still in English.

## Kept on purpose

Some behaviour of the originals looks like a mistake but is kept because the games work that way:

- The Etherlords skills **Ethereal Attack** and **Ethereal Defense** do nothing.
- Random battle events of Etherlords never happen, as in the original.
- Several Etherlords global spells only cost upkeep.
- As in the originals, computer players never answer alliance or trade offers.

## Corrected card texts

The rules stay as in the originals, but where a card's text describes something else, the remake shows corrected text (in English and Russian; the game files are not changed):

- **Unsummon**, **Lesser Unsummoning** and **Greater Unsummoning** return creatures to their owner's hand, even when another hero controls them.
- **Noise** can target either hero, and the **Evil Eye Warrior**'s gaze can target your own creatures too.
- **Madness**, **Irritation**, **Insanity** and **Snake Venom** hurt a hero at the end of that hero's own turns only. Insanity also blocks the creature's abilities.
- Effects "until end of turn" from **Fog**, **Ash Storm**, **Feedback**, **Reflection**, **Ethereal Resonance**, **Assault**, **Alpha Strike**, **Bless**, **Martyrdom**, **Seal** and **Ripple** last until the end of the opponent's turn, so cast in your own turn they also cover the opponent's next turn.
- **Wall of Distortion** stops creatures from attacking rather than preventing damage.
- **Second Birth** returns a creature that costs exactly X.
- **Fury of Nature** (Etherlords) counts every creature it strikes, including those that survive. **Boon of Nature** repairs only your own castle. Global spells that do nothing say so.
- Creatures: the **Vampire Bat** flies; a **Velos** survives if it was damaged at any time since its controller's previous turn ended; boosts of the **Walls of Lava, Inferno Flames and Wind**, the **Phantasmal Wall** and the **Wizard Spirit** last until the end of the opponent's turn; the **Fire Elemental**'s burn lasts until the end of its controller's turn; the **Mother Bee**'s frenzy and the **Kobold Elder** affect only friendly creatures; **Hoppers** rise only at the end of their owner's turn; the **Mantis Scribe** and **Mantis Patriarch** count only untapped targets; the **Lamia Warlock**'s controller draws.
- **Pale Scourge** can prey on Walls and heals by the prey's remaining toughness, the rewards of the **Pale Scourge**, **Reaper** and **Slayer** need the prey to die, and the **Pale Avenger**, **Revenant** and **Redeemer** give up a random spell (Etherlords II).
- **Lock Aspect** hurts the creature's owner, **Flux** counts your creatures that are not Pale, and **Dissolution** needs at least 1 ether, which it does not spend (Etherlords II).
