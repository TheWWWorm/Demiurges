# Expedition writing and its wiki sources

This document maps the current Expedition writing to the repository's English and Russian wiki. It covers the 19 encounter scenes, 38 event endings, 12 passive relics, four original keystones, 12 study paths, 13 scouting descriptions, and the ten guardian powers added in rules version 13. The Shrine of Knowledge scene and early Spirit offers were added in rules version 14. Version 15 adds the Artifact Constructor encounter and a six-recipe selection of charged artifacts, with linked assembly and recharging services.

The encounters, items, study labels, and journey are **new Expedition fiction set in the Etherlords world**. The wiki supplies the peoples, places, customs, objects, and printed magic. It does not describe these particular meetings, NPCs, transactions, or passive items. An original object used as an inspiration is not a claim that its campaign effect has changed.

The mode's gold economy, active-book range, reserve, recipes, persistent relics, and guardian-reward system are Expedition rules. They are not presented here as the original adventure rules. For the actual mode rules, see [the expansion guide](expedition_expansion.md).

## Source policy

- Use `wiki/en` and `wiki/ru` for setting and story. Read both editions instead of translating proper names by guesswork.
- Use the exported pages in `public/wiki/data/en/pages` and `public/wiki/data/ru/pages` for displayed spell, creature, skill, rarity, and ability terms. These exports are built from the repository's wiki/catalog material.
- Prefer the Etherlords II column when a card or skill changed between games. Keep Etherlords I-only references identified as older-world inspirations.
- Do not invent dialogue for a named campaign hero or transfer their canonical deeds to a random Expedition starter. The new NPCs below remain unnamed.
- Keep exact costs, caps, targets, and consequences in the choice or ability rules. Scenes and endings establish the situation; receipts report the resource changes.

No wiki article or exported catalog is changed by this writing pass.

### Early Spirit Covenant offers in version 14

The first two Spirits, WRSP and SHSP, both cost four ether in the printed catalog. Rules 14 permit those two cards in Spirit Covenant's Act I focus slot and prefer an eligible one until the active book or reserve contains a Spirit. Their printed cost and effects do not change, and the player still takes only one reward. The ordinary reward slots and other schools keep their existing preferences. Versions 1–13 retain the three-ether Act I focus ceiling.

This is an offer rule for assembling an existing Kinet strategy sooner, not a new clan or a rewritten spell. WRSP and SHSP provide Regeneration, which can trigger Life Channel. The Kinet Ancestral Tomb is an existing setting reference for returning warriors, not a claim that this Expedition school recreates that map object's effect. ## Version 13 guardian powers

The ten powers use the exact English/Russian titles of existing spells or a hero skill. Their new part is **Expedition acquisition and preparation**: an active enchantment power begins a duel already attached, or Concentration supplies at least the advanced skill rank. These rewards are not new canonical spells, renamed specializations, or changes to the shared card rules.

Opening enchantments use the normal duel effects and can be dispelled. Attaching them is not a cast and does not itself spend a book charge or trigger a cast effect. A draw does not create new spell charges. Concentration's advanced rank is the catalog's second rank (internal index 1), with an existing higher rank preserved. ## Version 15 artifact projects

The original Etherlords II system supports blank artifacts, constructed artifacts, and charges that persist after a battle. The Artifact Recipe map object supplies construction instructions; the Artifact Recharger restores charges. The new Constructor encounter adapts those ideas into a journey decision. Its English title does not identify a previously documented campaign building, and its Russian title, **Мастерская артефактов**, describes the new workshop. Original object names remain **Artifact Recipe / Рецепт артефакта** and **Artifact Recharger / Реконструктор Артефактов**. Sources: [map objects, EN](../wiki/en/map-objects.md) / [RU](../wiki/ru/map-objects.md); [runes and artifacts, EN](../wiki/en/runes-artifacts.md) / [RU](../wiki/ru/runes-artifacts.md).

The six recipes retain their original spell names and Etherlords II effects. They use the existing custom-artifact activation path, rather than adding a spell page to the book. Activation spends no ether or hand card, uses the original spell's target rules, and counts toward the hero's shared artifact allowance. That allowance is one activation per duel without Artificer, or two, four, or eight with its three ranks. The new recipes each hold two journey charges, have a one-round interval after use, and become available in the attack phase from the round shown below. Their capacity and timing are Expedition recipe choices; they do not alter the printed spells. The original artifact rules and Artificer behavior remain in the duel engine.

Custom-artifact spells still trigger spell traps and Data Attraction. **Spell Translocation / Транслокация заклинаний** does not grant its extra draw from an artifact cast. Purify still supports **Selective Purification / Тщательная очистка**. **Expert Artificer / Эксперт в артефактах** can preserve the charge, but that activation still counts against the shared per-duel limit. The skill's larger allowance and the specialization's chance to preserve a charge are separate effects. The comparison and Deck UI explain these interactions before commitment. Taking a project packs unfinished parts; its ending therefore makes no claim that the hero has already assembled or charged a usable artifact. A previously completed artifact remains usable while the new project is carried. Completion replaces that artifact and discards its remaining charges. Taking a finished artifact as an elite reward also replaces the carried artifact, but leaves a different unfinished project intact. These single-slot ownership rules belong to Expedition.

Camp assembly and recharging consume the camp's main action. Haven assembly costs 28 gold before merchant modifiers; a merchant recharge costs 20 before modifiers and restores one charge once per visit. The Reliquary can instead restore missing charges for three life each. Those costs, the salvage payment, and their scene endings are mode rules. In particular, the original campaign Recharger's recharge-all interaction is unchanged. The source spell remains inspectable under its original name, while the travel UI shows the artifact's charges and timing instead of misleadingly presenting a spell's printed ether price as the activation cost.

## Maintenance checks

When changing this writing again:

- Check both language pages for every named spell, skill, creature, or place. English and Russian titles are not always literal equivalents.
- Retain original card IDs and rules when changing a description. Describe an existing mechanic before attaching a new story explanation to it.
- Keep the invented NPCs and objects distinct from campaign heroes, unique sanctuaries, and original artifact entries.
- If a new event changes costs or destinations, update its choice text, scene ending, and this mapping together. Do not let a scene promise a required fight, a free service, or a physical destination that the choice does not provide.
- Preserve the distinction between an Expedition power being prepared for future duels and an already-started duel's saved state. A prose change must not imply reapplying a dispelled effect or refilling charges on reload.
